It began after watching [The Coding Train](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9zfeTw-uFCw). I really wanted to try it out, and made a simple simulator. These little dudes have a dense NN brain, with sigmoid activation functions. Their brains get selected and mutated based on how much food they ate. Over a few generations, they get pretty good at finding all the food.

</MediaRow>

After wanting to expand their brains, I decided to just continue with pure ML. I found the MNIST dataset. It is a collection of handwritten digits, 0-9. I had to write a full backpropagation method, and add training methods, new activations like argmax, and a few other functions. But that allowed a 70% accuracy after just a couple thousand trainings!

</MediaRow>

That led me to RNNs, or Recurrent Neural Networks. They essentially cycle back the last piece of data they gave out, and so they can 'remember' data. Here it can learn a simple word, and can predict the next letter that comes after.

</MediaRow>

<MediaRow
  src="/images/legacy/neural-network-library/04.webp"

I thought RNNs were neat, but not neat enough. They couldn't remember branching paths, like any words that had double letters. So I tried out LSTMs! Or Long Short-Term Memory cells. They're more complicated than RNNs, but doable. I wrote the code, the backpropagation code, and the required activations and other functions. Now it can remember the entire alphabet, or a simple sentence! Amazing!

</MediaRow>

I wanted to get a better score on the MNIST dataset, so I set out to learn how CNNs work. They're quite complicated, but after a while, and after finding [this paper](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Derivation-of-Backpropagation-in-Convolutional-(-)-Qi/5d7911c93ddcb34cac088d99bd0cae9124e5dcd1) which put all the equations in simple terms, I managed to write all the backpropagation code, maxpool layers, dropout layers, and all the matrix math behind it. Now I get a 92% accuracy after just half an epoch! (Side note: I was using a rather slow laptop at the time, and this isn't a speedy library by any means, so training is really slow. I couldn't be bothered to train for more than that.)

</MediaRow>
